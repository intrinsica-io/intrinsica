Advertising Strategy Review

Review of Intrinsica_Advertising_Strategy.html against the live site (intrinsica.io) · 1 October 2026

Verdict: the strategy is sound and its core idea is right. But the site can't yet support three things the plan relies on, and two risks aren't covered. Fix the blockers before spending the first €100, otherwise the money buys traffic you can't learn from.

What's strong in the strategy

  • "Advertise the insight, not the product" and "the stock analysis is the acquisition unit". “Is NVDA overvalued?” beats “try my SaaS”.
  • Staged spend (€100–150 to learn, then decide) and “don't launch ten channels at once”.
  • Main KPI = paid-plan intent per qualified visitor, not CTR or followers. That matches what the fake door was built to measure. The plan names match the site (Free / Pro / Unlimited).
  • The “What NOT to do” list is excellent, especially “don't advertise the technology” and “don't send every ad to the homepage”.

Blockers: the site doesn't support the plan yet

1No attribution: UTMs are not captured BLOCKER

What a UTM is: a label added to the end of a link that tells your site where the visitor came from. The page itself doesn't change.

https://intrinsica.io/?utm_source=x&utm_campaign=nvda_post
https://intrinsica.io/?utm_source=google&utm_campaign=fair_value_ads
https://intrinsica.io/?utm_source=reddit&utm_campaign=valueinvesting

utm_source = which channel (x, google, reddit…); utm_campaign = which ad or post. Google, X and Reddit Ads can add these automatically. Attribution = knowing which ad produced which result. If €50 on Google and €50 on X lead to 4 Pro clicks, you need to know where those 4 came from to decide where the next €100 goes.

The problem today: the site records page_view with no source information. The events sheet looks like this:

TimeEventVisitor
14:02page_viewv-81f…
14:03plan_selected (Pro)v-81f…

After the fix, the site reads the UTM labels and the referring website on arrival, remembers them for the visit, and adds them to every event:

TimeEventVisitorSourceCampaign
14:02page_viewv-81f…xnvda_post
14:03plan_selected (Pro)v-81f…xnvda_post

Effort: small (a few hours). Do this first; it makes even free posts measurable.

2No ticker landing pages BLOCKER

The plan's best tactics (“NVDA intrinsic value” → the NVDA analysis, Reddit “See NVDA's analysis →”, ticker-specific Google keywords) need links like intrinsica.io/t/NVDA, each with its own X preview card. Today every ad lands on the homepage with AAPL, which the plan itself says not to do. This is the shareable-links project; it becomes a prerequisite for paid Stage 2.

Effort: roughly one to two days.

3Retargeting needs pixels and cookie consent DEFER

Stage 5 needs X, Reddit or Meta tracking pixels. In the EU, pixels require prior consent (a cookie banner), and the site has none. Recommendation: skip retargeting during the smoke test; your own events sheet is enough.

4The example uses the wrong scales FIX

The strategy's sample post shows “Moat: 92/100” and “Risk/Reward: 1.6×”. The site shows Moat on 0–10 and Reward/Risk as a tier + score. Ads and posts must match exactly what the click lands on, or trust drops on arrival.

Risks the strategy doesn't cover

5Data licence: ads make the Yahoo question sharper

Posting “NVDA: Quality 9.4, Fair Value $185” in paid ads is unambiguously commercial use of Yahoo-derived numbers, a higher risk than a quiet test page. Options:

  • Cautious: keep creatives free of numbers. Lead with the question and the method; numbers appear only on the site.
  • Pragmatic: accept the risk knowingly for a short campaign.

6Financial-content rules

Per-stock scores and “fair value” figures in public posts can count as investment recommendations under EU rules (MAR), which require disclosures. Google also requires financial-services verification in many countries. At minimum, every post and ad should say “Not investment advice / for educational purposes”, matching the site footer. (Not legal advice; worth a short check before the paid stage.)

7The budget is too small to give a reliable answer

The chain from money to answer; every step loses people:

€ spent  →  clicks (visitors)  →  people who run an analysis  →  people who click a paid plan
StepTypical numberWith €75 on Google
Cost per click (finance keywords)€1–5+, because banks and brokers bid on the same words≈ 15–75 visitors
Paid-plan clicks in a fake-door test1–3 in every 100 visitors≈ 0–2 paid clicks

Why 0–2 tells you nothing: it's like flipping a coin twice and concluding it always lands heads. With numbers that small, luck dominates, so you can't tell “bad idea” from “unlucky week”. What you need: roughly 300+ visitors per channel before judging it.

Getting there without a bigger budget:

  1. Cheaper, more specific keywords: “NVDA fair value”, “is AMD overvalued” rather than “stock analysis”. Fewer bidders, higher intent (needs the ticker pages from blocker 2).
  2. Cheaper channels: X and Reddit clicks cost less than Google, and free posts count toward the 300 too, as long as UTMs show where they came from.
  3. Rules written down before spending, e.g. “after 300 visitors from a channel: ≥ 2 paid clicks per 100 who ran an analysis → keep going; fewer than 1 per 200 → stop that channel.”

8Free channels are under-used

Reddit's investing subs mostly ban self-promotion, so “participate genuinely” is right but slow. Faster free places for a launch:

  • Hacker News “Show HN”, Indie Hackers, r/SideProject, Product Hunt
  • Finance-newsletter and small-YouTuber outreach
  • The methodology itself as content: “every input and weight is on show” is the differentiator, and it attracts the analytical audience most likely to pay.
